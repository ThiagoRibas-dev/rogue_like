# Action Types
## Category
Actions in Combat
## Summary
An action’s type essentially tells you how long the action takes to perform within the 6-second combat round and how movement is treated.
## Mechanics
There are six types of actions: standard actions, move actions, full-round actions, free actions, swift actions, and immediate actions.
In a normal round, you can perform a standard action and a move action, or you can perform a full-round action. You can also perform a swift action, an immediate action (subject to availability), and as many free actions as the DM allows. You can always take a move action in place of a standard action.
In some situations (such as in a surprise round), you may be limited to taking only a single move action or standard action (plus any allowed free, swift, or immediate actions). See [surprise.md](./surprise.md).

### Standard Action
A standard action allows you to perform an act that takes a substantial amount of time. The most common standard action is a single melee or ranged attack. Other common standard actions include casting a spell, concentrating to maintain an active spell, activating a magic item, and using a special ability. See [actions-in-combat.md](./actions-in-combat.md) for a full list.

### Move Action
A move action allows you to move your speed or perform an action that takes a similar amount of time. You can move your speed, climb or swim part of your speed, draw or stow a weapon or other item, stand up, pick up an object, or perform some equivalent action.
You can take a move action in place of a standard action. For example, rather than moving and attacking, you could stand up and move (two move actions). If you move no actual distance in a round (commonly because you have swapped your move for one or more equivalent actions), you can usually take one 5-foot step before, during, or after the other actions you're taking. See [take-5-foot-step.md](./take-5-foot-step.md) and [tactical-movement.md](./tactical-movement.md).

### Full-Round Action
A full-round action consumes almost all your effort during a round. The most common full-round action is a full attack, which allows you to make multiple melee or ranged attacks in a single round. Before, during, or after a full-round action in which you don't otherwise move, you can usually take a 5-foot step. Some full-round actions don't allow a 5-foot step. Other full-round actions can be started or completed as standard actions in situations when you're limited to performing only a standard action during your turn (such as during a surprise round).

### Free Action
Free actions consume a very small amount of time and effort. Their impact is so minor that you can perform one or more free actions during your turn. However, the DM can put reasonable limits on what you can really do for free (such as speaking too much or performing too many quick tasks).

### Swift Action
A swift action consumes a very small amount of time, but it represents a larger expenditure of effort than a free action. You can take a swift action any time during your turn, but you can perform only one swift action per turn.

### Immediate Action
An immediate action consumes a tiny amount of time. Unlike a swift action, an immediate action can be performed at any time during a round, even when it isn't your turn.
*   **Resource Cost:** Using an immediate action on your turn counts as your swift action for that turn. If you use an immediate action when it isn't your turn, you cannot use another immediate or swift action until after your next turn.
*   **Restrictions:** You cannot use an immediate action when you are flat-footed.

### Not an Action
Some activities are so minor that they aren't even considered free actions. They literally don't take any time at all to do and are considered an inherent part of doing something else (such as making a Concentration check or guiding a mount with your knees).

### Restricted Activity
A few situations (such as a surprise round or being slowed) make you unable to take a full round's worth of actions. In such cases, you are allowed to take only a single standard action or a single move action, along with allowed swift, immediate, and free actions. You can't take a full-round action and finish it in the same turn, but you can start or complete a full-round action by using a standard action. See [start-complete-full-round-action.md](./start-complete-full-round-action.md).

## Special Notes
Action types dictate the action economy of a round. Swift and immediate actions represent a modern addition to the 3.5e ruleset, allowing characters to cast quickened spells, activate certain magic items, or perform class-specific features without disrupting their main standard/move actions.
