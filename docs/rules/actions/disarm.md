# Disarm

This material is Open Game Content, and is licensed for public use under the terms of the Open Game License v1.0a.

## Category

Special Attack

## Summary

This rule describes the disarm special attack, allowing a character to knock a weapon from an opponent's hands or, if unarmed, take possession of it.

## Mechanics

### Disarm Attempt

-   **Action Type:** Melee attack.
-   **Target:** Can be used to disarm a melee weapon or other items.
-   **Step 1: Attack of Opportunity:** You provoke an attack of opportunity from the target. If this attack deals any damage, your disarm attempt fails.
    -   If you have the Improved Disarm feat, you do not provoke an attack of opportunity from the target.
-   **Step 2: Opposed Rolls:** You and the defender make opposed attack rolls with your respective weapons.
    -   **Modifiers:**
        -   Wielder of a two-handed weapon gets a +4 bonus.
        -   Wielder of a light weapon (including unarmed strike) takes a -4 penalty.
        -   Larger combatant gets a +4 bonus per difference in size category.
        -   If the targeted item is not a melee weapon, the defender takes a -4 penalty on the roll.
-   **Step 3: Consequences:**
    -   **Success Outcome:** If you beat the defender, they are disarmed.
        -   If you attempted the disarm unarmed, you now have the weapon.
        -   If you were armed, the defender's weapon is on the ground in their square.
    -   **Failure Outcome:** If you fail, the defender may immediately react and attempt to disarm you with the same opposed melee attack roll. Their attempt does not provoke an attack of opportunity from you. If they fail, you do not get a free disarm attempt against them.

### Special Notes

-   **Spiked Gauntlets:** A defender wearing spiked gauntlets cannot be disarmed.
-   **Locked Gauntlet:** A defender using a weapon attached to a locked gauntlet gets a +10 bonus to resist being disarmed.

### Grabbing Items

-   You can use a disarm action to snatch an item worn by the target. If you want the item in your hand, the disarm must be made as an unarmed attack.
-   If the item is poorly secured or easy to snatch/cut away, the attacker gets a +4 bonus.
-   Failing this attempt does not allow the defender to attempt to disarm you in return. Otherwise, it functions identically to a disarm attempt.
-   You cannot snatch a well-secured item unless you have pinned the wearer (see Grapple). Even then, the defender gains a +4 bonus on their roll to resist the attempt.
