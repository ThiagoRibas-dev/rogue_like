# Attacks of Opportunity
## Category
Combat Basics

## Summary
The combat rules assume combatants are actively defending themselves. Sometimes, however, a combatant lets her guard down. When this happens, nearby opponents can take advantage of this lapse in defense to make a free melee attack, known as an attack of opportunity (AoO).

## Mechanics

### 1. Threatened Squares
You threaten all squares into which you can make a melee attack, even when it isn't your turn.
*   **Armed Requirement:** You must be considered armed to threaten squares (having a melee weapon, unarmed strike feat, natural weapon, or gauntlet). Unarmed characters who do not possess natural weapons or feats (like Improved Unarmed Strike) do not threaten squares and cannot make attacks of opportunity.
*   **Reach:**
    *   **Medium or Smaller:** Typically have a reach of 5 feet, threatening only adjacent squares (including diagonals).
    *   **Reach Weapons:** Double the threats of a typical character of that size (e.g., Medium wielders of reach weapons threaten 10 feet but do not threaten adjacent squares).
    *   **Large or Larger:** Have a natural reach of 10 feet or more, threatening all squares within that reach.
    *   **Tiny or Smaller:** Have a natural reach of 0 feet. They do not threaten any squares around them and must enter a foe's square to attack, provoking an AoO in doing so. See [big-and-little-creatures-in-combat.md](./big-and-little-creatures-in-combat.md).
*   **Obstructions:** Cover and total concealment prevent you from making attacks of opportunity against creatures in affected squares. See [cover.md](./cover.md) and [concealment.md](./concealment.md).

### 2. Provoking an Attack of Opportunity
An opponent provokes an attack of opportunity from you if they perform a provoking action inside a square you threaten. There are two categories of provoking actions:
*   **Movement:** Leaving a threatened square.
    *   *Exceptions:* Taking a 5-foot step ([take-5-foot-step.md](./take-5-foot-step.md)) or performing a withdraw action ([withdraw.md](./withdraw.md)) does not provoke.
    *   *Single Opportunity per Move:* Moving out of multiple squares threatened by the *same* opponent in the *same* round does not count as more than one opportunity for that opponent.
*   **Focused Actions:** Diverting attention from defense to perform an action (e.g., casting a spell, using a ranged weapon, drinking a potion, retrieving an item).
    *   *Exceptions:* Some actions, like casting a spell, allow a check (e.g., casting defensively) to avoid provoking.
    *   See [actions-in-combat.md](./actions-in-combat.md) for a comprehensive list of actions and whether they provoke.

### 3. Making the Attack
*   **No Action:** An attack of opportunity is not an action. It is a single melee attack made at your highest normal attack bonus.
*   **Timing (Interrupts):** An AoO interrupts the normal flow of actions. Resolve the AoO immediately when provoked, then resume the provoking creature's turn.
*   **Flat-Footed Restriction:** You cannot make attacks of opportunity if you are flat-footed, unless you have the Combat Reflexes feat.
*   **Frequency:** You can make only one attack of opportunity per round, unless you have the Combat Reflexes feat.
*   **Combat Reflexes Feat:**
    *   Allows you to make a number of additional attacks of opportunity per round equal to your Dexterity modifier.
    *   Allows you to make attacks of opportunity while flat-footed.
    *   *Limits:* You still cannot make more than one attack for a *single given opportunity*. If an opponent provokes two separate opportunities (such as moving and then casting a spell), you can make two separate attacks of opportunity, using up two of your attacks of opportunity for the round.

## Special Notes
*   **AoO Chain:** If you make an attack of opportunity that itself would normally provoke (such as making an unarmed strike without the Improved Unarmed Strike feat), your attack of opportunity provokes attacks of opportunity from opponents who threaten you.
*   **Entering Spaces:** You usually provoke an attack of opportunity when entering a foe's space (e.g., during a grapple, bull rush, or overrun).
