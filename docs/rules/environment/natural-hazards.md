# Natural Hazards

Nature itself is sometimes a formidable obstacle, or even a serious threat to life and limb.

---

## Altitude

High altitude can be extremely fatiguing—and sometimes deadly—to creatures that aren't used to it. Cold becomes extreme, and the lack of oxygen in the air can wear down even the hardiest warriors.

### Acclimated Creatures
Creatures that are accustomed to high altitude fare better than those that aren't. Any creature whose Environment entry mentions or includes mountains is considered native to the area, and thus acclimated to the high altitude. Creatures can also acclimate themselves by living at high altitude for a month or by acquiring a related feat or ability. Those who spend more than two months away from the mountains must reacclimate themselves when they return.
*   **Immunity:** Undead, constructs, and other creatures that don't breathe are immune to altitude effects.

### Altitude Zones
Mountains present three possible altitude bands:
1.  **Low Pass (lower than 5,000 feet):** Most travel in low mountains takes place in low passes. The altitude has no game effect.
2.  **Low Peak or High Pass (5,000 to 15,000 feet):** All nonacclimated creatures labor to breathe in the thin air. Such creatures must succeed on a Fortitude save each hour (DC 15, +1 per previous check) or become **fatigued**. The fatigue ends when the creature descends to an altitude where more air is present.
3.  **High Peak (more than 15,000 feet):** At these elevations, creatures are subject to both high altitude fatigue and altitude sickness, whether or not they're acclimated. Altitude sickness represents long-term oxygen deprivation:
    *   After each 6-hour period spent at this altitude, a creature must succeed on a Fortitude save (DC 15, +1 per previous check) or take 1 point of damage to all ability scores.
    *   Acclimated creatures receive a +4 competence bonus on their saving throws to resist these high altitude effects.

## Avalanches

Heavy snowfalls or loose rock can trigger deadly avalanches. 
*   **Snow and Ice Avalanches:** Advance at a speed of 500 feet per round.
*   **Earth and Rock Avalanches:** Advance at a speed of 250 feet per round.

### Avalanche Zones
An avalanche consists of two distinct areas:
1.  **Bury Zone:** The direct path of the falling debris. It is half as wide as the avalanche's full width.
2.  **Slide Zone:** The area the debris spreads out to encompass.
*   **Width:** A typical avalanche is $1d6 \times 100$ feet wide.
*   **Positioning:** To determine the precise location of creatures in the path, roll $1d6 \times 20$ feet from the center of the bury zone.

### Sensing an Avalanche
*   **Spotting:** Can be spotted up to $1d10 \times 500$ feet downslope with a Spot check (DC 0, but the avalanche has a +16 size bonus, meaning a Net DC of -16, though distance penalties apply: -1 per 10 feet).
*   **Listening:** With no other loud noises, a DC 15 Listen check hears an avalanche when it is $1d6 \times 500$ feet away.
*   **Failure:** If all creatures fail their checks, they automatically become aware of it when it closes to half the original distance.

### Avalanche Effects
*   **Bury Zone:** Creatures take 8d6 points of damage (Reflex DC 15 half) and are buried.
*   **Slide Zone:** Creatures take 3d6 points of damage (Reflex DC 15 negates). Those who fail the save are buried.
*   **Debris:** Both zones are filled with heavy debris (difficult terrain).

### Buried State
*   Buried creatures take 1d6 points of nonlethal damage per minute.
*   If a buried creature falls unconscious, it must succeed on a DC 15 Constitution check or take 1d6 points of lethal damage each minute thereafter until freed.

### Digging Out
*   **By Hand:** In 1 minute, a rescuer clears debris equal to five times their maximum load. (Loose stone in a 5-ft square weighs 2,000 lbs; snow/ice weighs 400 lbs).
*   **With Tools:** A shovel or appropriate tool allows clearing material twice as fast.
*   **Self-Rescue:** A buried creature can free itself with a DC 25 Strength check (DM's discretion).

## Cave-Ins

A weakened ceiling might collapse when subjected to a major impact or concussion. By destroying half the pillars holding up a weakened ceiling, a creature can cause a cave-in. 
*   **Detection:** A weakened ceiling can be spotted with a DC 20 Knowledge (architecture and engineering) check or a DC 20 Craft (stonemasonry) check.
*   **Effects:** A typical cave-in has a bury zone (15-foot radius) and a slide zone (10-foot-wide band surrounding the bury zone). Damage and burial follow the same rules as an avalanche.

## Lava

Lava deals intense fire damage:
*   **Exposure/Contact:** 2d6 points of fire damage per round of exposure.
*   **Total Immersion:** 20d6 points of fire damage per round.
*   **Residual Heat:** Damage from lava continues for 1d3 rounds after exposure ceases, but at half the rate (1d6 for exposure, 10d6 for immersion).
*   **Immersion Hazard:** A creature immune to fire might still suffocate if completely immersed in lava (see Suffocation below).

## Quicksand

Patches of quicksand present a deceptively solid appearance. A typical patch is 20 feet in diameter.
*   **Detection:** A creature approaching at normal speed is entitled to a DC 15 Survival check to spot it. Charging or running creatures get no check and sink $1d2 \times 5$ feet into it.
*   **Quicksand Effects:**
    *   Creatures must succeed on a DC 10 Swim check every round to stay in place, or a DC 15 Swim check to move 5 feet.
    *   If a trapped creature fails this check by 5 or more, it sinks below the surface and must hold its breath (see Suffocation).
    *   To swim back to the surface, a submerged creature must make a Swim check (DC 15, +1 per consecutive round submerged).
*   **Rescuing Victims:** A rescuer needs a tool/branch/rope to reach the victim. The rescuer makes a DC 15 Strength check, and the victim makes a DC 10 Strength check to hold on. If both succeed, the victim is pulled 5 feet closer to safety.

## Smoke

Smoke obscures vision and hampers breathing.
*   **Thin Smoke:** Obscures all sight (including darkvision) beyond 60 feet. Creatures separated by more than 30 feet have concealment (20% miss chance). Creatures separated by 60 feet have total concealment (50% miss chance). Breathing thin smoke has no mechanical effect.
*   **Thick Smoke:** Grants concealment to creatures 5 feet away; creatures farther away have total concealment.
    *   A creature breathing thick smoke must succeed on a Fortitude save each round (DC 15, +1 per previous check) or spend that round coughing and choking.
    *   Choking for 2 consecutive rounds deals 1d6 points of nonlethal damage.

## Water Hazards

*   **Calm Water:** Wading in relatively calm water that isn't over a creature's head requires no checks. Deep water requires a natural swim speed or Swim checks.
*   **Cold Water:** Freezing water deals 1d6 points of nonlethal cold damage per round. A creature taking this damage is **fatigued**.
*   **Deep Water (Pressure):** Deep water deals 1d6 points of damage per minute for every 100 feet a creature is below the surface. A Fortitude save (DC 15, +1 per previous check) negates the damage for that minute. Aquatic creatures are unaffected until they reach 500 feet, and take only 1d6 points of damage per 200 feet below that depth.
*   **Flowing Water:** Floating objects or creatures move downstream at 10 to 40 feet per round (rapids send them downstream at 60 to 90 feet per round). 
    *   Swept away by a stream moving 60 ft/round or faster: Must succeed on a DC 20 Swim check each round to avoid going under.
    *   Making the check by 5 or more allows catching a stationary object to stop movement.
    *   Even on a successful Swim check, rapids deal 1d3 points of nonlethal damage per round (1d6 lethal damage if flowing over rocks).
*   **Floods:** River width increases by $1d4 \times 50\%$, depth increases by 1d10+10 feet, and Swim check DCs increase by one step (e.g., calm becomes rough). Flow speed increases by 50%.
*   **Flash Floods:** Torrential rain can raise water levels by 1d10+10 feet in minutes. Water behaves like rapids. Creatures struck by the initial wave must succeed on a DC 15 Reflex save or take 3d6 points of bludgeoning damage.

## Wildfires

Wildfires advance 120 feet per round in moderate winds.
*   **Detection:** Spot check (DC 0, but +16 size/light bonus, meaning Net DC -16, with distance penalties) at up to $2d6 \times 100$ feet. Blinded creatures can feel the heat at 100 feet.
*   **Ambient Heat:** Unprotected creatures (protection level 1 or lower) take 1d6 points of fire damage and 1d4 points of nonlethal damage per round (no save). Wearers of metal armor are affected as if by a *heat metal* spell. Partially protected creatures (protection level 2 or 3) take this damage once every 10 minutes.
*   **Catching on Fire:** Creatures at risk must succeed on a DC 15 Reflex save or catch fire, taking 1d6 points of fire damage per round. In each subsequent round, a DC 15 Reflex save must be made; failure means another 1d6 fire damage, success extinguishes the flame. Jumping into water or rolling on the ground (+4 bonus) extinguishes the fire.

## Starvation and Thirst

Creatures without food or water suffer fatigue, damage, and death. Damage from starvation/dehydration cannot be healed (naturally or magically) until the creature has been properly fed or rehydrated.
*   **Lethal Damage Conversion:** A creature that falls unconscious from nonlethal damage due to starvation or thirst begins taking the same amount of lethal damage instead.

### Starvation
*   **Food Requirements:** A Medium creature needs 1 lb of food per day. (Scale: $1/2 \times$ per size category smaller, $8\times$ per size category larger).
*   **Going Without:** A creature can go without food for 3 days. Afterwards, it must succeed on a Constitution check each day (DC 10, +1 per previous check) or take 1d6 points of nonlethal damage.
*   **Fatigue:** Creatures that take nonlethal damage from lack of food become **fatigued**.
*   **Treatment:** Must eat before any damage can be healed.

### Thirst
*   **Water Requirements:** A Medium creature needs 1 gallon of fluids per day. (Scale: $1/2 \times$ per size category smaller, $4\times$ per size category larger).
    *   *Hot Climates (above 90° F):* Double normal requirements. Add $+1\times$ requirements per temperature band higher than hot.
*   **Going Without:** A creature can go without water for a number of hours equal to 24 + its Constitution score (reduced in hot environments). Afterward, it must succeed on a Constitution check each hour (DC 10, +1 per previous check) or take 1d6 points of nonlethal damage.
*   **Fatigue/Dehydration:** Creatures taking nonlethal damage from lack of water are dehydrated and **fatigued**. If a dehydrated creature would take nonlethal damage from hot environmental conditions, that damage becomes lethal instead.

| Temperature Band | Temperature Range | Time Before Con Checks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal** | Under 90° F | 24 + Con hours |
| **Hot** | 91° to 110° F | 12 + Con hours |
| **Severe/Extreme Heat** | 110° to 180° F | 3 + Con hours |
| **Burning Heat** | 181° F or higher | 1/2 Con hours |

### Treating Dehydration
*   **Nonlethal damage:** Requires a DC 15 Heal check, 24 hours of care, and double the normal water required for the climate.
*   **Lethal damage:** Requires a DC 20 Heal check, 48 hours of care, and double the normal water.
*   **Magical:** A *heal* spell rehydrates a creature instantly, bypassing recovery times and checks.

## Suffocation

*   **Holding Breath:** A creature can hold its breath for 2 rounds per point of Constitution, provided it takes only move, free, swift, or immediate actions. Taking a standard or full-round action reduces the remaining time by 1 round.
*   **Constitution Checks:** Once the limit is reached, the creature must make a DC 10 Constitution check each round to continue holding its breath. The DC increases by 1 for each previous success.
*   **Suffocating State:** Upon failing a check, the creature begins to suffocate:
    1.  **Round 1:** The creature falls unconscious and drops to 0 hit points.
    2.  **Round 2:** The creature drops to -1 hit points and is **dying**.
    3.  **Round 3:** The creature suffocates and dies (-10 hit points).
*   **Preparing to Hold Breath:** As a move action, a creature can prepare by deep breathing. A successful DC 15 Swim check adds 4 to the number of rounds they can hold their breath, if done immediately.

### Slow Suffocation
*   **Air Volume:** A Medium creature breathes easily for 6 hours in a sealed 10-ft cube. Afterward, it takes 1d6 points of nonlethal damage every 15 minutes.
*   **Scale:** Smaller creatures need half as much air per category smaller; larger creatures need four times as much per category larger.
*   **Fires/Additional People:** Each additional person or major fire source (e.g., a torch) reduces the time the air lasts proportionally.
*   **Unconsciousness:** Falling unconscious from slow suffocation immediately drops the creature to -1 hit point (dying) on the next round, and suffocates them (-10 hit points) on the following round.

## Falling

Characters who fall from heights suffer impact damage.

### Standard Falling
*   **Damage:** 1d6 points of damage per 10 feet fallen, up to a maximum of 20d6.
*   **Deliberate Jumps:** A character who deliberately jumps down can make a DC 15 Jump check or DC 15 Tumble check. Success treats the first 10 feet of the fall as nonlethal damage.
*   **Tumble check:** A character trained in Tumble can make a DC 15 Tumble check to treat the first 10 feet of a fall as nonlethal damage. This is cumulative with a deliberate jump (reducing the first 20 feet of a fall to nonlethal damage if both succeed).
*   **Yielding Surfaces:** Falling onto yielding surfaces (like mud, hay, or a canopy) treats the first 1d6 of damage as nonlethal. This is cumulative with deliberate jumps and skill checks.

### Falling Into Water
*   If the water is at least 10 feet deep, the first 20 feet of falling deal no damage.
*   The next 20 feet deal nonlethal damage (1d3 per 10-foot increment).
*   Beyond 40 feet, the fall deals 1d6 points of lethal damage per 10 feet.
*   **Deliberate Dives:** A character diving into water takes no damage on a successful DC 15 Swim check or DC 15 Tumble check, provided the water is at least 10 feet deep for every 30 feet fallen. The DC of the check increases by 5 for every 50 feet of the dive.

### Falling Objects
Falling objects deal damage based on weight and distance fallen. An object must fall at least 10 feet to deal damage, up to a maximum of 20d6.

*   **Heavy Objects (200+ lbs):** Deals 1d6 points of damage per 200 lbs, plus an additional 1d6 points of damage for every 10-foot increment it falls beyond the first 10 feet.
*   **Light Objects (under 200 lbs):** Must fall further to deal 1d6 points of damage. Use the table below for the damage increment.
*   **Objects under 1 lb:** Deal no damage, regardless of distance.

| Object Weight | Minimum Drop Distance for 1d6 Damage |
| :--- | :--- |
| **200 lbs or more** | 10 feet (plus 1d6 per 200 lbs) |
| **101 to 199 lbs** | 20 feet |
| **51 to 100 lbs** | 30 feet |
| **31 to 50 lbs** | 40 feet |
| **11 to 30 lbs** | 50 feet |
| **6 to 10 lbs** | 60 feet |
| **1 to 5 lbs** | 70 feet |
