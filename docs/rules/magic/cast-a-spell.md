# Cast a Spell
## Category
Standard Actions
## Summary
Most spells require 1 standard action to cast, allowing you to cast them either before or after a move action.
## Mechanics
Most spells require 1 standard action to cast. You can cast such a spell either before or after you take a move action. You retain your Dexterity bonus to AC while casting.
### Spell Components
- **Verbal (V):** Requires speaking in a firm voice. Cannot cast if gagged or in a *silence* spell area. Deafened spellcasters have a 20% chance to spoil spells with verbal components.
- **Somatic (S):** Requires free gestures with at least one hand. Cannot cast while bound, grappling, or with both hands full or occupied.
- **Material (M), Focus (F), or Divine Focus (DF):** Requires proper materials as described by the spell. Preparing unlisted materials is a free action if you have a spell component pouch.
- **Experience Point (XP):** Entails an XP cost. Lost XP cannot be restored. You cannot spend so much XP that you lose a level, but you can spend XP on casting a spell immediately upon gaining enough XP to achieve a new level, rather than keeping it to advance. XP is expended whether casting succeeds or fails.
### Concentration
You must concentrate to cast a spell. If something interferes with your concentration, you must make a Concentration check (DC depends on threat) or lose the spell. If failed, prepared spells are lost, and at-will spells count against daily limits.
### Concentrating to Maintain a Spell
Concentrating to maintain a spell is a standard action that doesn’t provoke an attack of opportunity. Anything that could break your concentration when casting a spell can keep you from concentrating to maintain a spell. If your concentration breaks, the spell ends.
### Casting Time
Most spells have a casting time of 1 standard action and take effect immediately.
### Attacks of Opportunity
Generally, casting a spell provokes attacks of opportunity from threatening enemies. If you take damage from an attack of opportunity, you must make a Concentration check (DC 10 + points of damage taken + spell level) or lose the spell. Spells that require only a free action to cast don’t provoke attacks of opportunity.
### Casting on the Defensive
Casting a spell while on the defensive does not provoke an attack of opportunity. It requires a Concentration check (DC 15 + spell level). Failure means you lose the spell.
### Touch Spells in Combat
Many spells have a range of touch. You cast the spell and then touch the subject, either in the same round or any time later. In the same round, you may attempt to touch the target. You can move before casting, after touching, or between casting and touching. You automatically touch one friend or yourself. To touch an opponent, you must succeed on an attack roll.
- **Touch Attacks:** Touching an opponent with a touch spell is an armed attack and does not provoke attacks of opportunity (though casting the spell does). Touch attacks can score critical hits. The opponent's AC against a touch attack does not include armor, shield, or natural armor bonuses; size, Dexterity, and deflection bonuses apply normally.
- **Holding the Charge:** If you don’t discharge the spell in the round it was cast, you can hold the charge indefinitely, making touch attacks round after round. Touching anything (even unintentionally) discharges the spell. Casting another spell dissipates the touch spell. Alternatively, you can make a normal unarmed attack (or natural weapon attack) while holding a charge; if it hits, you deal normal damage and the spell discharges. If it misses, you still hold the charge.
### Dismiss a Spell
Dismissing an active spell is a standard action that doesn’t provoke attacks of opportunity.
## Special Notes
Spellcasting is a complex action with various rules regarding components, concentration, and interactions with combat.
