# Magic Items (General Rules)

Magic items are essential assets for adventurers. Using them requires understanding how to identify them, where to wear them, and how to activate their powers.

---

## Identifying Magic Items

When characters find magic items, they must determine their functions, command words, or remaining charges through one of the following methods:

### Skills
*   **Spellcraft:** 
    *   *Detect Magic:* A Spellcraft check (**DC 15 + 1/2 item's caster level**) identifies the magic aura's school and strength. If the check exceeds the DC by **10 or more**, the creature learns the item's functions, activation method, and remaining charges.
    *   *Potions:* A **DC 25 Spellcraft check** identifies a potion. The check takes 1 minute, and failure cannot be retried.
*   **Search:** A **DC 15 Search check** can reveal hidden physical clues, such as command words etched in tiny lettering on a ring or a descriptive design.
*   **Knowledge (Arcana):** A **DC 30 check** allows identifying historically legendary items or items with traditional shapes.
*   **Use Magic Device (UMD):** Succeeding on a UMD check to activate an item and beating the DC by **5 or more** divines the item's functions, activation method, and remaining charges.
*   **Bardic Knowledge:** Bards can recall details about items:
    *   *DC 20:* Standard item of a well-known faction.
    *   *DC 25:* Uncommon or ancient item, but similar to others in circulation.
    *   *DC 30+:* Known only in legend. A success yields history and hints.

### Spells
*   ***Detect Magic*:** Identifies magic auras (see Spellcraft above).
*   ***Identify*:** Determines functions, activation, and charges of non-artifact items. Takes 1 hour to cast and costs 100 gp in material components.
*   ***Analyze Dweomer*:** Faster and more reliable than *identify* (no expensive material component, allows identifying multiple items in a single casting).

## Wearing and Carrying Magic Items

### Body Slots
A normal humanoid creature has **twelve body slots**. Only **one active magic item** can be worn in each slot. If multiple items are worn in the same slot, only the first one donned functions.

1.  **Arms:** Armbands, bracelets, bracers.
2.  **Body:** Armor, robes.
3.  **Face:** Goggles, lenses, masks, spectacles.
4.  **Feet:** Boots, sandals, shoes, slippers.
5.  **Hands:** Gauntlets, gloves.
6.  **Head:** Circlets, crowns, hats, helmets.
7.  **Rings (2 slots):** Rings (one per hand/ring slot).
8.  **Shoulders:** Capes, cloaks, mantles, shawls.
9.  **Throat:** Amulets, badges, brooches, necklaces, pendants, scarfs.
10. **Torso:** Shirts, tunics, vests, vestments.
11. **Waist:** Belts, sashes.

*   **Matched Pairs:** Some slots represent pairs (Hands, Feet). An item worn on only one part (e.g., a *glove of storing*) still consumes the entire slot. Items that come in pairs must be worn together to function.
*   **Attunement:** Some items must be worn for a certain length of time (usually 24 hours) before their powers can be accessed, preventing swapping them mid-day.

### Nonworn Magic Items
Carried or held magic items (like *ioun stones* or *rods*) must be carried somewhere on the person to function, but they fail to work if placed inside extradimensional storage (such as a *bag of holding*).

### Size, Shape, and Nonhumanoid Limits
*   **Armor & Shields:** Do not resize to fit different size categories. Nonhumanoid-shaped creatures cannot wear humanoid armor.
*   **Weapons:** Do not resize, but can be wielded with standard size penalties (see [weapons.md](../weapons.md)). Magic properties function as long as they can be held.
*   **Limb Restrictions:**
    *   *Armless:* Lose arms, hands, and rings slots.
    *   *Fingerless:* Lose rings slots.
    *   *Headless:* Lose face, head, and throat slots.
    *   *Legless:* Lose feet slot.
    *   *Multilegged:* Treat foremost pair of legs as arms (gaining arms, hands, and rings slots).

## Activating Magic Items

If an item's description does not specify an activation method, assume a **Command Word** is required.

### 1. Command Word
Requires the user to speak the activation word aloud.
*   **Silence:** Blocked in areas of magic silence, or if the user is unable to speak.
*   **Action:** Takes the same time as the casting time of the spell duplicated (or a **Standard Action** if it doesn't duplicate a spell). Does not provoke attacks of opportunity.

### 2. Continuously Functioning
Donned items that apply their benefits continuously.
*   **Action:** No action to activate once worn. Donning the item takes at least a **Standard Action** (provoking attacks of opportunity) or longer (e.g., armor).

### 3. Manipulation
Requires physical movement (e.g., pulling a patch from a *robe of useful items* or drinking a potion).
*   **Free Hand:** Requires a free hand (similar to a somatic spell component).
*   **Action:** Usually a **Standard Action** (or as described). Activating a manipulation item as a move, standard, or full-round action **provokes attacks of opportunity**.
*   **Disruption:** Taking damage or being grappled while activating a manipulation item forces a **Concentration check** (DC 10 + damage taken + spell level, if applicable). Failure wastes the activation.
*   **Potions & Oils:** Corporeal creatures use them as a standard action. Administering a potion or applying an oil to an unconscious creature requires a **Full-Round Action**.

### 4. Mental
Purely mental activation (willing the item to function).
*   **Grapples/Silence:** Can be activated while grappled, pinned, paralyzed, or in a silence zone, provided the user has control of their thoughts.
*   **Incapacity:** Cannot be activated if the user is unconscious, sleeping, petrified, or dominated (unless ordered to do so by the dominator).
*   **Action:** Time equals duplicated spell's casting time (or standard action). Does not provoke attacks of opportunity.

### 5. Spell Completion (Scrolls)
The spell is already written and mostly finished; the user performs the final casting steps.
*   **Deciphering:** Scroll must be deciphered first (*read magic* or **Spellcraft DC 20 + spell level**).
*   **Activation:** Takes the spell's normal casting time, **provokes attacks of opportunity**, and is subject to arcane spell failure and concentration disruption.
*   **Requirements:** To activate a scroll, the user must:
    1.  Have the spell on their class spell list.
    2.  Possess the correct type (arcane scroll for arcane casters, divine scroll for divine casters).
    3.  Have a key ability score equal to at least **10 + spell level**.
*   **Caster Level Check:** If the user meets all requirements but their caster level is lower than the scroll's, they must succeed on a caster level check (**DC = scroll CL + 1**) to activate it.
    *   *Failure:* If the CL check fails, the user must make a **DC 5 Wisdom check** to avoid a **mishap** (uncontrolled damage, spell targeting the wrong creature, or random delays). A natural 1 on the CL check always fails.

### 6. Spell Trigger (Wands and Staffs)
The item stores a spell that requires a simple activation word.
*   **Class List:** The spell must be on the user's class spell list (even if they cannot cast spells of that level yet).
*   **Action:** Casting time of the stored spell. **Does not provoke attacks of opportunity**. Silence or inability to speak blocks activation.
*   **Appendix (Wands vs. Staffs):**
    *   *Grappling:* Wands can be activated in a grapple. Staffs cannot.
    *   *Staff Scaling:* Staffs are unique: they use the wielder's caster level, ability scores, and feats (like Spell Penetration or Spell Focus) to calculate save DCs and checks if those values are higher than the staff's base values.

## Limited Uses and Depletion

*   **Charged / Single-Use:** Have a set number of charges (usually 50 for wands/staffs). When depleted, the item becomes a permanent, nonmagical object. Partially depleted items cannot be recharged.
*   **Daily-Use:** Function a set number of times per day. Unless noted otherwise, they regain all daily uses at **dawn**.
